# V27 economic and market-structure hypothesis

## Mechanism written before local V27 counts or P&L

The FX market is closed for a material part of the weekend while information continues to arrive. Liquidity is initially thin when trading resumes and participants must incorporate accumulated information discontinuously. An extreme Friday-close-to-Monday-open move can therefore contain both fundamental repricing and a temporary overreaction component. If the temporary component is material, taking the opposite side of only the most extreme historical gaps and holding through the following trading week should earn a reversal premium.

Dao, McGroarty and Urquhart, “A calendar effect: Weekend overreaction (and subsequent reversal) in spot FX rates,” *Journal of Multinational Financial Management* 37-38 (2016), 158-167, DOI `10.1016/j.mulfin.2016.11.001`, reports reversal after large weekend gaps across major and emerging currencies. Its out-of-sample rule uses the top and bottom 5% of a five-year weekend-gap history, trades opposite the gap, and uses a one-week horizon. The authors report robustness to transaction costs and interest rates. Source record: `https://irep.ntu.ac.uk/id/eprint/35555/`.

V27 adopts that published 5%/95% rolling-tail and one-week contrarian structure. Broker-server timestamps, delayed entry after the opening-liquidity shock, the mandatory protective stop, risk sizing, execution controls and acceptance gates below are prospective engineering choices. They are not selected from local V27 return observations.

## Falsifiable expectation

After realistic spread, commission, swap and execution friction, independently extreme upward and downward foreign-currency weekend gaps should produce positive one-week contrarian expectancy in both the 2025 and pre-seal 2026 Development partitions. Failure of any frozen mandatory gate retires V27; no threshold, timing, universe, stop or cost parameter may be changed after results are viewed.
