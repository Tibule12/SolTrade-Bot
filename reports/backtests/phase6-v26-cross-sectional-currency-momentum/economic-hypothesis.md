# Cross-sectional currency momentum hypothesis

## Prediction

At weekly horizons, currencies that appreciated most against the U.S. dollar over the preceding three completed weeks will, as a portfolio, continue to outperform currencies that depreciated most over the next completed week.

This is a cross-sectional, approximately dollar-neutral relative-strength hypothesis. It does not predict that an individual exchange rate crossing an absolute boundary will trend. The signal requires dispersion and ranking across a fixed currency universe; an individual currency can enter the long portfolio while falling in absolute terms if it is relatively strongest.

## Ex ante rationale

The rationale is investor underreaction and gradual incorporation of currency-specific information, with limits to arbitrage preventing immediate elimination of the relative-return spread. Published evidence reports significant cross-sectional currency momentum, distinct from carry and benchmark technical rules, and reports that three-week formation with one-week holding produced strong returns in a broad currency sample.

Primary research basis:

- Menkhoff, Sarno, Schmeling and Schrimpf, “Currency momentum strategies,” *Journal of Financial Economics* 106(3), 2012, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2012.06.009
- Raza, Marshall and Visaltanachoti, “Is there momentum or reversal in weekly currency returns?”, *Journal of International Money and Finance* 45, 2014, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jimonfin.2014.02.009

## Separation from retired families

V26 uses no Donchian channel, breakout boundary, continuation candle, retest, EMA, ATR regime, or V1–V3 losing-trade rule. It uses no London-fix entry or post-fix reversal premise. The published three-week/one-week horizon is selected before broker data qualification and before any candidate return, rank, signal count, or P&L is inspected.

This document freezes the economic rationale only. Universe membership and operational rules will be frozen after availability-only broker-data qualification and before any price-return calculation.
